The Math of a Former Ghost Town

Let’s look at the numbers. Dayr Jamal is a compact 1.5 km² town sitting strategically on the international highway, 32 km (20 miles) north of Aleppo and 26 km south of the Turkish border.

Before the war, it was a thriving agricultural hub of 21,547 people (pre 2016 census), famous for its olive groves, grain harvests, and 'Pylon' (a natural clay used in traditional soap and shampoo). It was a quiet, productive, conservative community.

Then the math turned brutal:

April 2013

Heavy regime airstrikes crippled the civilian infrastructure.

June 2015

A relentless barrel bomb campaign caused documented massacres, forcing the population into a mass exodus.

February 2016

The town was occupied by SDF/YPG militias. The residents were pushed into displacement camps, and Dayr Jamal was hollowed out.

And we mean literally hollowed out. Occupying forces spent eight years digging vast networks of subterranean tunnels beneath the civilian streets, transforming a farming village into a fortified military depot. Homes were seized, vineyards were stripped to their iron frames, and the town was suffocated.

The return

The Brutal Reality of the Return

Fast forward to today. The occupation has ended, and the original residents are flooding back. But they are returning to a devastated grid.

To understand why rebuilding this mosque is so critical, you have to understand what these people are enduring right now:

200 Ldiesel daily

The Power Grid is Dead

There is no state electricity. The local council tried to run massive diesel generators left behind by the militias, but pumping water for just 12 hours requires over 200 litres of diesel daily. At a cost of hundreds of dollars a day, the fuel ran out fast.

20 kmnight drive

A Total Medical Collapse

The town’s only health centre is destroyed. Recently, a 9-year-old boy suffered a severe midnight asthma attack. His parents tried to run his medical nebuliser on a weak solar battery, but it died. With no local doctors, they had to make a desperate, terrifying 20 km night drive to Azaz just so their son could breathe.

9 yrsin tents

The Trauma of the Tents

Ask any returning mother why she is choosing a house with a collapsed roof and no doors over a refugee camp. The answer is blunt. For nine years in places like the Shamarekh camp, families froze in the winter and suffocated on the toxic fumes of burning nylon bags used for cheap heating. To them, a shattered, freezing concrete wall in their hometown is a luxury compared to the indignity of the canvas tent.

Why Ibadur Rahman Masjid? The Highway Multiplier

A town without an Adhan is not a town; it is just a collection of ruins.

33%deficit
3 mosques · 21,547 residents
33% deficit in congregational space
Two other mosquesIbadur Rahman Masjid

Pre-2016, Deir Jamal’s 21,547 residents were served by exactly three mosques. Today, the Ibadur Rahman Masjid lies in ruin. That is a 33% deficit in congregational space, leaving the entire southern sector of the town with no masjid within a 20-minute radius.

But Ibadul Rahman has a massive geographic advantage. It sits directly on the major international highway connecting Aleppo to Turkiye, Azaz, and Northern Syria.

Because it is highly visible from the main road, this mosque does not just serve the locals. It acts as the primary spiritual rest stop for thousands of daily travellers, truck drivers, and displaced families moving along this critical artery. Every single wayfarer (Ibn Al-Sabil) who stops to pray, rest, or make Wudu multiplies your reward. You are securing Hasanat from both a recovering local population and a massive transit demographic.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Donate & Rebuild
Why focus on the Ibadul Rahman Masjid?

Geography and immediate need. This masjid serves the entire southern sector of Deir Jamal, along with a major travel highway. With it destroyed, returning residents in this area are left completely cut off, with absolutely no other mosque within a 20-minute radius. Rebuilding it anchors the southern grid and brings the Adhan back to a massive portion of the population.

Where exactly is Deir Jamal?

It is strategically located 32 km north of Aleppo and 26 km south of the Turkish border, directly on the international highway. The mosque’s location is 36°26'25.3"N 37°01'43.4"E

Is the town actually safe enough to rebuild?

Yes. The eight-year occupation by militia forces has ended. The underground military tunnels have been cleared by local forces, and the original residents are actively leaving displacement camps to return to their homes. The rebuilding phase has already begun.

What exactly does the $124,000 USD cover?

Physical, ground-up reconstruction. This includes rubble removal, structural masonry, repairing the collapsed minaret, installing the electrical grid, setting up the sound system for the Adhan, and laying down the interior prayer carpets.

Why is there a hard deadline for Ramadan?

These families spent the last nine Ramadans freezing in canvas tents. The goal is brute-force acceleration to get the walls up and the carpets down so the residents can pray Taraweeh in their own town this year for the first time in a decade. Urgency drives results.

Does this count as Zakat or Sadaqah?

Building a mosque falls under Sadaqah Jariyah (continuous charity). Zakat has strict categories of recipients. This campaign is an investment for your Akhirah that will continue generating reward for you long after you are gone.

What happens if the campaign exceeds the $124,000 goal?

Any surplus funds will be routed directly into sustaining the mosque's operational costs—such as fuelling generators for electricity and water pumps—or distributed to the returning families struggling to repair their shattered homes.
